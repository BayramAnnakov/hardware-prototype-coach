# Bill of materials and cost

A person who has seen the layout usually asks next what it costs. Answer with a table they can check, not a single number.

## The table

One row per part, in the person's language:

| Part | Role | Qty | Unit price | Line total | Source, date read |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

- Link the exact product page you opened for each price, and the date you read it. A search-result snippet is not a source.
- Mark every price you did not open as an estimate. Fasteners, feet, gasket sheet, wires and keycaps are commonly estimates.
- Note stock when the page shows it; an out-of-stock part needs a second source.
- Packs: list the pack, not the unit you need (switches in tens, inserts in hundreds).
- A part replaced during the session leaves the table; check the CAD envelope still matches the part that replaced it. In one session a bought expander board was 43 × 18 mm against an assumed 30 × 20 mm envelope.

## The total

Give three separate lines and a range, not one figure:

1. Parts: sum the low and high ends of the rows.
2. Shipping: count the stores; small orders from several stores carry several fixed fees.
3. Made parts (printing, cutting): a quote ties to exact files. Without a quote, scale a dated quote of a similar object by volume and call it an estimate; price does not scale exactly with volume.

Say what is not included: taxes and duties, tools, a second print after a failed fit.
